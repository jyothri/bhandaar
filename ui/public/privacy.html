Bhandaar

Privacy Policy

Effective 2 October 2026

Bhandaar (sm.jkurapati.com) is a private storage analyzer. It shows you what takes up space in your Google Drive, Gmail, Google Photos and Google Cloud Storage, and on your own drives. It is run by Jyothrilinga Kurapati (“I”, “me”) for personal use and invited users. There is no public sign-up: every account is created by me.

This policy explains what data Bhandaar accesses, why, how it is stored and protected, who it is shared with (no one), and how you can have it deleted. Questions or requests: bhandaar-contact@googlegroups.com.

In short: Bhandaar reads only metadata (names, sizes, dates and similar), only from the Google services you choose to connect, and only with read-only permissions. It never reads the contents of your email, files or storage objects, never changes or deletes them, never shows ads, and never sells, shares or transfers your data.

1. Google user data Bhandaar accesses

You connect a Google account to Bhandaar one service at a time, through Google’s own consent screen. Bhandaar asks only for the permissions (OAuth scopes) that service needs, and every one of them is read-only.

Permission (scope) What Bhandaar reads Why
openid, email (every connection) Your Google account’s ID and email address. To tell your connected accounts apart and to recognise an account connected again. Only a masked form of the address (for example jyo****ri@gmail.com) is stored and shown.
gmail.readonly (Gmail) For each message: its ID and thread ID, labels, the From, To, Subject and Date headers, and its size. To list your largest and oldest messages, so you can see what uses your mailbox storage.
drive.metadata.readonly (Google Drive) For each file and folder: its name, ID, folder, type, size, modified time and MD5 checksum. To show your Drive as a folder tree with the size of each folder.
photospicker.mediaitems.readonly (Google Photos) Only the photos and videos you pick yourself in Google Photos’ picker: each item’s ID, file name, type, when it was taken, dimensions, camera and exposure details, and its size. To show how much space the items you picked take up.
devstorage.read_only and cloudplatformprojects.readonly (Google Cloud Storage) Your Cloud project names and IDs; each bucket’s name, location and settings (storage class, versioning, soft delete); and for each object: its name, size, storage class, version, checksums and dates, including older versions and recently deleted objects. To show your buckets as folder trees, with what each folder costs to store, including storage that normal listings hide.

What Bhandaar does not access

  • The body or attachments of any email, the contents of any Drive file, or the contents of any Cloud Storage object.
  • Any photo or video you did not pick. For the ones you pick, Bhandaar asks Google for each item’s size. In the rare case Google doesn’t give one, it downloads the item once to count its bytes and compute a checksum, then discards it; the photo itself is never stored. Bhandaar stores no location data.
  • Your contacts, calendar, or any Google service you haven’t connected.

Bhandaar never changes or deletes your email, files, photos or storage objects. The only thing it creates in your Google account is a temporary Google Photos picking session when you choose to pick photos, which it deletes once your pick is scanned.

2. How Bhandaar uses this data

Only to show you your own storage: scan results, folder trees, totals and lists of your largest items, on pages that only you can see after logging in. Specifically, Bhandaar does not:

  • sell, rent or share your data;
  • use it for advertising, or for profiling you;
  • use it to develop, improve or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models;
  • let any person read it, except you, and me when you ask for help with your account, when needed for security, or to comply with the law.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Bhandaar’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

3. Sharing

Bhandaar does not share, sell or transfer Google user data to anyone. It runs on a server I own and control; no hosting provider, analytics service or other third party receives it. Bhandaar sends requests to Google’s own APIs to read your data, as described above. The only exception would be a disclosure required by law.

4. Other information Bhandaar keeps

  • Your Bhandaar login: a username, and a password stored only as a salted Argon2id hash. Failed login attempts are recorded briefly to block password guessing.
  • Connection details for each Google account: the access and refresh tokens Google issues, and the permissions you granted. The refresh token lets Bhandaar run the scans you request later without asking you to sign in again.
  • Scan history: when each scan ran, what it covered (for example a Drive folder or a Cloud Storage bucket), and its totals.
  • Local drives: if you use Bhandaar’s driveagent tool, the file names, folders, sizes, dates and checksums of the drives you scan with it. This is not Google data.
  • Server logs: technical records of requests and errors, used only to run and fix the service.

5. Cookies and browser storage

Bhandaar sets one cookie, bhandaar_session, to keep you logged in. It is HttpOnly (scripts can’t read it), sent only to Bhandaar, and expires 30 days after you last use the site, or when you log out. Your browser also keeps a few settings for Bhandaar (such as the account and service you last browsed), and, only while you connect an account, a random security value for Google’s sign-in. There are no advertising or analytics cookies, and no tracking.

6. How data is stored and protected

  • Data is stored in a database on my own server, reachable only over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS). Access to the server and database is limited to me.
  • Each user sees only their own scans and connected accounts; the server checks this on every request.
  • Passwords are stored only as Argon2id hashes; repeated failed logins are blocked.
  • Google tokens are kept on the server and are never sent to your browser.

7. How long data is kept

  • Scan results are kept until you delete the scan.
  • Your Drive and Cloud Storage folder trees are kept, and updated by each new scan, until you disconnect the Google account in Manage data, or ask me to remove it.
  • Connection details and tokens are kept until you disconnect the Google account in Manage data, or ask me to remove it. If you revoke Bhandaar’s access at Google, the tokens stop working at once.
  • Login sessions end 30 days after last use, or when you log out.
  • Google Photos picking sessions are deleted at Google as soon as the scan of what you picked is done.

8. Your choices, and deleting your data

  • Connect only what you want. Each Google service is connected separately, and you can decline any permission on Google’s consent screen.
  • Delete scans yourself, in Bhandaar’s Request History. This deletes the scan and what it found.
  • Revoke access at any time from your Google Account, at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Bhandaar then can no longer read anything from that account.
  • Disconnect a Google account yourself, in Bhandaar’s Manage data page. This revokes Bhandaar’s access at Google and deletes all data kept for the account, including its tokens, scans and folder trees, at once. You can also delete just one service’s data of an account there (Gmail, Google Drive, Cloud Storage or Google Photos).
  • Delete a drive uploaded with driveagent, as uploaded from one computer, in Manage data.
  • Remove your whole Bhandaar account, or anything else: email bhandaar-contact@googlegroups.com. I will delete it within 30 days and confirm by email.
  • Ask what data Bhandaar holds about you, or for a copy of it, at the same address.

9. Children

Bhandaar is not directed at children under 13, and accounts are created only by me.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date. If a change affects how your Google data is used, I will tell you before it takes effect, and ask for your consent again where needed.
